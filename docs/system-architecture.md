
# System Architecture

## 1. Overview

The Smart Refrigerator is an ESP32-based refrigeration prototype that combines environmental monitoring, door-status detection, thermoelectric cooling, and alternative power operation.

The system is organized into five main subsystems:

1. Control subsystem
2. Environmental monitoring subsystem
3. Door monitoring and alert subsystem
4. Thermoelectric cooling and air circulation subsystem
5. Power supply subsystem

## 2. System Operation

The ESP32 acts as the central controller.

The temperature and humidity sensor provides environmental measurements. These measurements are displayed on an LCD, allowing the user to monitor the refrigerator's internal conditions.

A magnetic sensor monitors the refrigerator door. When the door remains open longer than the configured time threshold, the ESP32 activates a buzzer to notify the user.

The cooling subsystem uses a Peltier thermoelectric module. Its hot side is connected to an external heat-dissipation assembly, while the cold side is coupled to an internal cooling assembly. An additional fan circulates the cooled air inside the refrigerator compartment.

The system receives power from a 12 V DC supply generated by an AC-to-DC SMPS or from a battery. The power source is selected manually.

## 3. Functional Subsystems

### 3.1 Control Subsystem

The ESP32 coordinates sensor monitoring, display updates, and door-open alert behavior.

### 3.2 Environmental Monitoring

The temperature and humidity sensor measures the internal environment. The LCD displays the measured values.

### 3.3 Door Monitoring

The magnetic sensor detects the door's state. If the door remains open for more than 20 seconds, the buzzer produces an alert. The alert timing is adjustable.

### 3.4 Cooling and Air Circulation

The Peltier module provides thermoelectric cooling. The external radiator dissipates heat from the hot side, while the internal cooling assembly transfers cooling into the refrigerator compartment.

A fan circulates air inside the compartment to improve the distribution of cooled air.

### 3.5 Power Supply

The system can operate using either an AC-powered 12 V DC supply or a battery. Switching between these sources is performed manually.

## 5. Design Considerations

- Continuous environmental monitoring
- Clear visual feedback through the LCD
- Audible notification for prolonged door opening
- Heat dissipation on the hot side of the Peltier module
- Air circulation inside the refrigerator compartment
- Alternative AC and battery power operation

## 6. Known Limitations

- The AC/battery power-source selection is manual.
- Quantitative cooling performance and energy-consumption results have not yet been documented.
- Exact control logic and sensor communication details should be confirmed against the firmware source code.
